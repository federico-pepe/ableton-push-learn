About

Ableton Push and Move Visualizer

I originally designed and developed this tool to solve a simple problem: I wanted to get better at playing Ableton Push in chromatic mode.

I'm Federico Pepe, an Ableton Certified Trainer, and I've always felt that Push is much more than a controller, it's a real instrument. The challenge is that, unlike most traditional instruments, there aren't many resources that clearly explain how to learn it. Most of what you find are diagrams of shapes or "tips and tricks." Those resources can be useful for some people, but they’re not how I learn. I need a way to understand the grid, study it over time, and practice it like I would with any other instrument.

That's where this project started.

At first, it was just a web app I built for myself, something to help me understand Push's grid better and practice in a more intentional way. Over time, it grew into something more complete and later, into a Max for Live device as well. That was also a learning journey for me: as this is the first Max for Live device that I developed, so while building a tool to learn Push, I ended up learning Max for Live too.

The project is open source and hosted on GitHub. Someone has already contributed improvements in the past, and anyone is welcome to do so.

Community support is the reason this tool exists at all. That's also why I want to keep it accessible, including a free option, so that anyone who's curious about Ableton Push or Ableton Move as an instrument can start learning, regardless of their budget.

I also see this as a valuable resource for educators who want to bring Push and Move into the classroom and use them as instruments for teaching music, not just production tools.

From a technical perspective, the tool is built using p5.js, webmidi.js, and tonal.js, which handles the automatic chord detection.
